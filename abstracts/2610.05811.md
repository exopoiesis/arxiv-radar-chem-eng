# Public Battery Field Data

**Authors:** Robert Masse

Battery systems and algorithms are developed and evaluated primarily on laboratory cycling data, but fielded battery systems are subjected to duty cycles, temperatures, and measurement conditions that cannot be reproduced in a lab. Field data from batteries in service has begun to appear in public releases attached to individual papers, but each release has its own format, units, labels, and other gaps that make it difficult to compare against others. We present a curated collection of 35 public field-data releases from 27 research groups, covering applications that span passenger EVs, buses, light electric mobility, grid-scale storage, distributed storage, consumer electronics, industrial robotics and satellites. Where the releases are complete enough to measure, this heterogeneous collection contains 256 GB of data from at least 149,188 lithium-ion cells, 3,187 batteries, 115.6 MWh of energy storage capacity, and 3,040 unit-years of service. Critically, it also contains 6,465 labeled or reported faults, which provide greater visibility into what real battery performance and safety issues look like outside the lab. We release a public repository of metadata and code for each of the field datasets. For each dataset it provides a registry entry with sources, license and access links, checksums to confirm a download is complete, a metadata table, and code for how to load it. We also report where released datasets differ from their papers. We hope the collection can help serve researchers (human or otherwise) as a useful platform for improving battery field diagnostics, fault detection and health estimation. Better field evidence can also feed back to improve manufacturing quality, inform second-life screening and residual-value estimates, and broadly accelerate electrification.
