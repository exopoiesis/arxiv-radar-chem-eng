# Real-time optimization with online learning for pressure swing adsorption

**Authors:** Carine de Menezes Rebello, Marcio Fontana, Leizer Schnitman, Idelfonso B. R. Nogueira

Real-time optimization (RTO) of pressure swing adsorption (PSA) units requires a process model that can be evaluated many times at each decision, which favours data-driven surrogates of the cyclic steady state. A surrogate, however, represents the plant only under the conditions covered by its training data, and disturbances that are not among its inputs remain outside its description. This work presents an RTO framework for PSA in which a neural network surrogate, optimized by particle swarm, is updated online with data generated at the plant when plant and model diverge. A rigorous dynamic model of a CH$_4$/CO$_2$ separation, integrated to cyclic steady state, served as virtual plant in three scenarios. In the first, five consecutive RTO events with different objectives ran without disturbance; all constraints were satisfied and the largest deviation between surrogate and plant was 1.7 percentage points. In the second, a step in the CO$_2$ feed fraction from 0.40 to 0.50 reduced the CH$_4$ purity at the operating point in force from 92.3 to 76.9%; after the network was fine-tuned on 43 local samples, the new RTO event returned a feasible optimum with a purity of 90.3%. In the third, a progressive loss of sorbent capacity was detected by the accumulated relative deviation of the CH$_4$ purity from a reference, triggering retraining before any constraint violation. Samples generated across the remaining activity range led to infeasible operation, whereas samples generated at the final activity kept the purity above 92.4% after the move transient.
