# TuneBench: Benchmarking AI Agents for Power System Dynamic Model Calibration

**Authors:** Andrea Pomarico, Salvatore Tessitore, Giorgio Giannuzzi, Alberto Berizzi

Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents can automate complex engineering workflows by interacting with tools, interpreting feedback, and making iterative decisions. This paper introduces TuneBench, a benchmark for evaluating Agentic AI in power system dynamic model calibration, where controller parameters are adjusted to reproduce target dynamic responses. The benchmark reflects a common Transmission System Operator (TSO) practice, in which equivalent models used for Dynamic Security Assessment (DSA) are calibrated against measurements from grid-code compliance tests when proprietary models are unavailable. Two cases of increasing complexity are considered: the governor and Automatic Voltage Regulator (AVR) of a synchronous generator and the control system of a Battery Energy Storage System (BESS), both tuned against active-power and voltage step tests within a budget of 15 simulations. LLM-based agents are compared with random search and Genetic Algorithm (GA) baselines over 10 independent runs. Only the most advanced models, Opus 5.5 and GPT-6, accurately reproduce all target trajectories, with median R2 above 0.995 in every test and tracking errors up to two orders of magnitude lower than the baselines. The agent logs show that these agents autonomously extract dynamic performance indices, such as settling time and damping, and relate them to specific control parameters, decomposing the tuning problem into physically interpretable sub-problems. The benchmark is publicly available at [https://github.com/Power-Agent/PowerAgentBench/tree/main/benchmarks/dynamic/level3](https://github.com/Power-Agent/PowerAgentBench/tree/main/benchmarks/dynamic/level3).
