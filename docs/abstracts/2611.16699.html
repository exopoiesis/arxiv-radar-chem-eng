Energy system device sizing under closed-loop operation with imperfect forecasts

Cara Koepele, Kevin Wallington, Georgios Mavromatidis, John Lygeros

arXiv:2611.16699·eess.SY·Published 2026-11-12

The value of renewable energy devices depends on their operation, which must adapt to weather and demand using limited, imperfect forecasts that update over time. Conventional energy systems planning practices assume unlimited foresight in a single-level formulation, potentially leading to long-term investment in suboptimal device sizes. Accounting for realistic operation corresponds to a bi-level problem: the upper level selects device sizes, while the lower level contains closed-loop operation under evolving forecasts. We solve this problem using backpropagation MPC (BP-MPC), a gradient-based method, with a deterministic MPC as the lower-level controller. Using forecast errors generated by a parameterized martingale model of forecast evolution, we investigate how forecast accuracy affects the benefits of operation-aware energy systems planning and the resulting device sizes. In a case study of an energy system comprising photovoltaics, a battery, a heat pump, and thermal storage with uncertain weather and energy demands, accounting for closed-loop operation yields higher average cost savings as forecast error increases. The resulting designs achieve these savings by progressively shifting investment from battery storage to solar generation and thermal storage, which buffers forecast mismatches.
