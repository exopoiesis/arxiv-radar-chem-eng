Reshaping Checkpoint-Induced Power Variations Through Inference Workload Scheduling

Yu Ma, Ziheng Sheng, Anna Andriiko, Chau-Wai Wong, Jong Ha Woo, Zishuo Yang, Aaditya Pandey, Wenyuan Tang, Ning Lu

arXiv:2611.12621·eess.SY·Published 2026-11-09

Synchronous checkpoints in large language model training cause rapid power variations that challenge frequency regulation in islanded data-center microgrids. This paper proposes a checkpoint-aware two-layer framework that schedules flexible inference workloads to compensate for checkpoint-induced disturbances. Using measured GPU power profiles and anticipated checkpoint power deficits, the upper layer coordinates heterogeneous task selection and launch times, while the lower layer adjusts the batch sizes of newly launched tasks online to correct power-tracking errors. Early launching (EL) and staged retirement (SR) further smooth power transitions at checkpoint onset and recovery, respectively. PSCAD/EMTDC simulations with reference checkpoint drops of 1-3 MW demonstrate reductions of 41.6-61.6% in maximum absolute frequency deviation and 68.5-89.6% in mean absolute frequency deviation relative to no scheduling. Battery energy storage system (BESS) peak charging power and charging energy decrease by 65.1-100% and 89.7-100%, respectively. These benefits exceed those of greedy scheduling when GPU resources are relatively abundant.
