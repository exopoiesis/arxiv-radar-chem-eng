Molecular Vibronic Complexity as a Quantum Resource for Optimal Control: Nonadiabatic Mixing Creates Protected Coherence Subspaces in Dissipative Environments

Elisa Palacino-González

arXiv:2610.15743·quant-ph·Published 2026-10-11

In molecules, electronic coherence typically decays within a few hundred femtoseconds, a timescale usually attributed to the environment. We show that it is set to a large extent by which coherences are excited: the molecule can host coherences that the bath barely sees, and optimal control can find and exploit them. Using gradient-based optimal control (GRAPE) with Redfield dynamics, we maximize the total vibronic coherence between the electronic ground and an optically bright electronic excited state in a three-state model with nonadiabatic features, in two regimes of low and high nonadiabatic mixing and for different system-bath coupling strengths. The Liouvillian spectrum reveals, in both regimes, a well-separated coherence mode with substantial ground-bright character that decays on a picosecond timescale. This mode is protected by mixing with the dark state, which brings the geometry of the optically accessible excited state close to that of the ground state and thus weakens its coupling to the bath. Without prior information, GRAPE finds this mode and keeps the photoexcited wave packet near the Franck-Condon region. At equal fluence, it increases the coherence at 800 fs by more than two orders of magnitude over a Gaussian pulse at the weakest coupling, and by many more at stronger coupling. The Liouvillian predicts the bath dependence of the long-lived coherence to within 1\%, and a Gaussian pulse tuned to the mode frequency comes within 10\% of the best pulse found. Beyond two-level and spin systems, our study puts forward the vibronic structure of molecules as a route to protected coherence subspaces, identifying a dark-state protection mechanism and a design criterion based on molecular parameters. These results lay the groundwork for our goal of using vibronic molecular systems as a resource for long-lived, controllable coherences in molecular quantum information science.
